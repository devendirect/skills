# Legal framework: European Union (common to the 27 member states)

What the skill may say about EU law, and nothing more. Each point quotes the official text, gives its source and the date it was checked. Anything marked *(to verify)* has **not** been checked at an official source: do not present it as a fact.

Directives are transposed by each member state with its own wording and details: see `legal-fr.md` (France) and `legal-de.md` (Germany). Other member states: the EU rules below apply, their national details are not covered. Regulations (GDPR, AI Act, Data Act) apply directly everywhere.

**We are not lawyers.** This file helps explain a verdict; it does not settle a legal question. Every answer ends with the disclaimer telling the user to have the project checked by a lawyer.

Last checked: **2026-10-04**. Sources and URLs: `sources.md`.

## 1. Database right (sui generis)

**Directive 96/9/EC** of 11 March 1996 on the legal protection of databases.

- Art. 1(2): a database is "a collection of independent works, data or other materials arranged in a systematic or methodical way and individually accessible by electronic or other means".
- Art. 7(1): the maker of a database which shows "a substantial investment in either the obtaining, verification or presentation of the contents" may "prevent extraction and/or re-utilization of the whole or of a substantial part, evaluated qualitatively and/or quantitatively, of the contents of that database".
- Art. 7(5): "The repeated and systematic extraction and/or re-utilization of insubstantial parts of the contents of the database implying acts which conflict with a normal exploitation of that database or which unreasonably prejudice the legitimate interests of the maker of the database shall not be permitted."
- Art. 8(1): a lawful user may extract or re-use "insubstantial parts of its contents, evaluated qualitatively and/or quantitatively, for any purposes whatsoever"; art. 15: a contractual provision contrary to art. 8 is "null and void".
- Art. 10(1): the right expires fifteen years from 1 January of the year following completion. Art. 10(3): "Any substantial change, evaluated qualitatively or quantitatively, to the contents of a database, including any substantial change resulting from the accumulation of successive additions, deletions or alterations, which would result in the database being considered to be a substantial new investment, evaluated qualitatively or quantitatively, shall qualify the database resulting from that investment for its own term of protection." A database kept up to date is therefore usually still protected.

**Data Act, Regulation (EU) 2023/2854**, art. 43: "The sui generis right provided for in Article 7 of Directive 96/9/EC shall not apply when data is obtained from or generated by a connected product or related service falling within the scope of this Regulation". It applies from 12 September 2025 (art. 50). This concerns data from connected devices (IoT), rarely what a scraper collects.

**In the skill**: O4 (`bulk`, or a volume of 1000 pages or more) and O5 (`republish`). The skill cannot tell whether the site made a "substantial investment" or whether a part is "substantial": it flags the risk, it does not decide it. For `monitor`, mention art. 7(5): repeated extraction of small parts can be caught too.

## 2. Contractual restrictions in terms of use

**CJEU, 15 January 2015, Ryanair Ltd v PR Aviation BV, C-30/14.** Ruling: Directive 96/9/EC "is not applicable to a database which is not protected either by copyright or by the sui generis right under that directive, so that Articles 6(1), 8 and 15 of that directive do not preclude the author of such a database from laying down contractual limitations on its use by third parties, without prejudice to the applicable national law."

What it does **not** say: that every ban in terms of use binds every visitor. In that case, access to the site "presupposes that the visitor to the site accepts the application of Ryanair's general terms and conditions by ticking a box" (point 16). Whether terms only linked in a footer, never accepted, bind a scraper is a question of national contract law *(to verify)*.

**In the skill**: a clause classified `prohibited` gives R4 (red) because the site has expressed a ban, whatever its exact legal force; the explanation stays cautious ("the terms seem to forbid…, their force depends on how they were accepted and on national law: ask a lawyer").

## 3. Text and data mining (TDM)

**Directive (EU) 2019/790** of 17 April 2019 on copyright and related rights in the Digital Single Market.

- Art. 2(2): TDM is "any automated analytical technique aimed at analysing text and data in digital form in order to generate information which includes but is not limited to patterns, trends and correlations".
- Art. 3: exception for research organisations and cultural heritage institutions, for scientific research, on works "to which they have lawful access". It cannot be reserved by the rightholder.
- Art. 4(1) and (3): exception "for reproductions and extractions of lawfully accessible works and other subject matter for the purposes of text and data mining", on condition that the use "has not been expressly reserved by their rightholders in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means in the case of content made publicly available online".
- Recital 18: for content made publicly available online, "it should only be considered appropriate to reserve those rights by the use of machine-readable means, including metadata and terms and conditions of a website or a service."

**What counts as "machine-readable" is disputed.** Recital 18 names "terms and conditions of a website", but a German appeal court held in December 2025 that a reservation on a website, which the first-instance court had described as written "(only) in natural language", did not have the required machine-readable form at the time of the download; a further appeal is allowed (`legal-de.md` § 2). The question is not settled at EU level. A case is pending before the Court of Justice: **C-250/25, Like Company v Google Ireland** (request from the Budapest Környéki Törvényszék, lodged 3 April 2025, OJ C/2025/3039). Among its questions: whether training an LLM-based chatbot "constitutes an instance of reproduction", and if so whether "such reproduction of lawfully accessible works fall[s] within the exception provided for in Article 4 of Directive 2019/790". No Advocate General's opinion or judgment had been published on 2026-10-04.

**AI models**: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act), art. 53(1)(c): providers of general-purpose AI models shall "put in place a policy to comply with Union law on copyright and related rights, and in particular to identify and comply with, including through state-of-the-art technologies, a reservation of rights expressed pursuant to Article 4(3) of Directive (EU) 2019/790". Chapter V applies from 2 August 2025 (art. 113(b)).

**In the skill**:
- R2 (TDMRep reservation): a technical, machine-readable signal, the safest kind.
- R3 (AI training crawlers blocked in robots.txt): whether this is an "appropriate" reservation is not settled *(to verify)*; the explanation says "the site objects", not "it is unlawful".
- R4 when a clause of the terms reserves TDM: red, because the site objects in writing; the explanation says its legal force as a reservation is disputed (recital 18 versus the Hamburg appeal decision), so the safe reading is to respect it.
- For `ai-tdm`, mention the research exception (art. 3) only if the user is a research organisation or a cultural heritage institution, and say it still requires lawful access.

## 4. Personal data (GDPR)

**Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR).**

- Art. 4(1): personal data is "any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person", for instance "by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier".
- Art. 6(1)(f): processing is lawful if "necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject". Other lawful bases exist (art. 6(1)(a)–(e)).
- Art. 14: information to give when data was not obtained from the person; art. 14(5)(b) allows an exception when it "proves impossible or would involve a disproportionate effort", with "appropriate measures … including making the information publicly available".

National data protection authorities publish their own guidance: for France, the CNIL's sheet on web scraping (`legal-fr.md` § 3). Other authorities *(to verify)*.

**In the skill**: O1 whenever personal data is likely; it is never covered, not even by an open licence, a public body or a permission in the terms. Say that the GDPR applies (lawful basis, information, minimisation), whoever scrapes, as soon as the data is about people in the EU.

## 5. Open licences

The skill only uses what the licence identifiers say, and it requires the licence to cover **the data**, not just the site's texts. Checked at the official pages:

- **Licence Ouverte / Open Licence 2.0 (Etalab)**: the reuser may, among others, "la communiquer, la reproduire, la copier ; … l'adapter, la modifier, l'extraire et la transformer … ; de la diffuser, la redistribuer, la publier et la transmettre", and exploit it commercially, provided they "mentionner la paternité de l'« Information » : sa source (a minima le nom du « Concédant ») et la date de la dernière mise à jour". It is designed to be compatible with licences that require at least attribution (CC-BY, ODC-BY).
- **ODbL** (summary page): free to share, create and adapt; conditions: attribute, share-alike ("If you publicly use any adapted version of this database, or works produced from an adapted database, you must also offer that adapted database under the ODbL"), keep open.
- **Creative Commons**: BY requires credit; SA requires derivatives under the same licence; NC excludes commercial use; ND excludes modified versions; CC0 waives rights (public domain dedication). The full legal codes were not read *(to verify before quoting them)*.

## 6. Not covered

- Copyright on individual contents (texts, photos) beyond the TDM exception: relevant for `republish`, *(to verify)* before saying more than "copyright applies unless a licence allows it".
- Unfair competition, parasitism, criminal law on intrusion into information systems: national law *(to verify)*.
