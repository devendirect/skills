Birdwatching on the Loire estuary

Fieldnotes is a weekly log of what can be seen between Saint-Nazaire and Paimbœuf, written by one birdwatcher who walks the estuary at dawn. Every note gives the date, the tide time, the place and the species seen, so you can plan a visit around the tide rather than the calendar.

The best months are October to March, when waders and wildfowl winter on the mudflats. High tide pushes them close to the dykes: arrive one hour before high water, stay one hour after. Spring brings the first spoonbills and avocets back; summer is quiet but good for terns.

Latest note

First light at Paimbœuf, 28 September 2026: 340 avocets, 12 spoonbills and the first brent geese of the season on the rising tide.

New notes are published every Monday. Sightings older than a year are archived but stay online.
